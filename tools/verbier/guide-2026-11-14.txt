Reader’s guide

This document consolidates the complete Verbier–4 Vallées game-world
research guide. It combines operator-published resort attributes, a
named feature inventory, the user-provided panoramic map, and practical
recommendations for building a geographically faithful snowboard game.

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Read before implementation. The piste artwork is not scale-accurate.
  Real lift alignments, piste centre-lines, cable heights, building
  footprints, snow depth and tree coordinates must be acquired from GIS
  data or survey; they are not silently invented here.

01 Scale, scope and accuracy rules

The 4 Vallées encompasses Verbier, La Tzoumaz, Bruson, Nendaz, Veysonnaz
and Thyon. The published domain total is approximately 410 km of runs
and itineraries and 82 ski lifts, with the highest served point at Mont
Fort, about 3,330 m. Counts vary with what an operator includes and with
infrastructure changes. The named inventories in this report are
deliberately partial extracts rather than a full 82-lift survey.

Primary build objectives

-   Preserve the relationships between actual valleys, ridges, lift
    endpoints and mountains instead of arranging disconnected slope
    props.

-   Build a 1:1 metre-based world wherever accurate GIS is available;
    distinguish visual backdrops from connected playable terrain.

-   Keep a dated, source-identified asset registry. Changes to lift
    infrastructure must be versioned, especially the new Savoleyres
    Télémixte.

-   Implement piste status, difficulty and ungroomed itinerary behaviour
    as data, not just texture colours.

02 Geography and terrain sectors

Verbier sits above the Val de Bagnes, with Le Châble in the valley
below. Bruson faces Verbier across the valley. Savoleyres is the ridge
link to La Tzoumaz, while the higher Mont Fort / Tortin corridor
connects toward Siviez and the wider Nendaz–Veysonnaz–Thyon network.
Mont Blanc and Grand Combin are iconic visual backdrops but are not
automatically piste-connected gameplay terrain.

03 Lifts, cable cars and the 2026/27 change

The real network mixes gondolas, detachable chairlifts, aerial
cableways, surface lifts, conveyor carpets and hybrid lifts. Each
requires a different interaction model in a game: vehicle type,
queueing, doors, loading and unloading, slope clearance, grip/haul
movement, station geometry and sensible route connectivity. Appendix B
lists 35 operator-inventory lift records, not the whole 4 Vallées
network.

Main named lift groups

Verbier

Le Châble–Verbier, Barnes Line and Médran 2 gondolas; Funispace; Mont
Gelé cableway; Chassoure; chairlifts Mayentzet, Attelas, Chaux 2 and Lac
des Vaux; Chaux-Express Télémixte; beginner surface lifts and carpets.

Savoleyres / La Tzoumaz

La Tzoumaz–Savoleyres gondola; Etablons, Taillay, Nord and Tournelle
chairlifts; Taillay and beginner connections; modern Piguet Galland
Télémixte.

Bruson

Le Châble–Mayens de Bruson gondola, Pasay chairlift and Moay /
Grand-Tsai surface lifts.

Mont Fort / 4 Vallées core

Jumbo, Tortin–Gentianes and Mont-Fort aerial cableways; Gentianes
surface lift; Tortin chairlift, Siviez–Plan du Fou gondola and Les
Chottes chairlift.

Esserts–Planards–Savoleyres Télémixte

The new hybrid link replaces the historical Verbier–Savoleyres system.
The upper Planards–Savoleyres section began operating in December 2025.
The lower Les Esserts–Planards section was scheduled for December 2026
at the research snapshot date; its completed operating status is not
independently confirmed in this document.

Recommended lift simulation

-   Define each line by surveyed bottom/middle/top station coordinates
    and cable profile; never approximate terminals from the panoramic
    illustration alone.

-   Use lift-specific vehicle geometry, loading cadence, speed and
    capacity attributes; do not assume that all gondolas travel at
    identical speeds.

-   Calculate catenary and tower clearance from engineering / survey
    sources or explicitly mark estimated clearances as placeholders.

-   Animate terminal arrival, deceleration, doors, boarding, chair
    pickup, dismount, queues, safety gates and weather shutdowns.

04 Pistes and difficulty classifications

Swiss piste colours are difficulty indications, not a reliable
substitute for measured slope angle. Width, grooming, shade, curvature,
traffic and snow condition affect the experienced difficulty. Yellow
itineraries are marked but normally ungroomed and must not be treated as
groomed pistes.

Signature slope characteristics

Attelas

An alpine descent around 2,700 m heading toward Mayentzet, incorporating
steeper transitions and a stated roughly 500 m vertical section.

Mont Fort

Steep black descent. The first roughly 250 m is reported near 35° and
can have major moguls; below this the terrain opens into broader groomed
skiing toward Col des Gentianes.

Pasay

A demanding Bruson black option; a very steep start is described around
35°, with a more gradual alternative around the hill and around 600 m
total vertical in the area.

Cœur / La Tzoumaz

Northern-aspect red terrain beginning near Savoleyres, with an easier
blue alternative; forest shadow and colder snow are characteristic
visual cues.

Individual feature dimensions, piste widths and precise gradient
profiles must be verified through digital terrain and linework, not
extrapolated from an overall piste colour.

Gentianes–Tortin

Start: Col des Gentianes (2,820 m stated); finish: Tortin (2,045 m

Terrain: long ungroomed mogul descent. Published distance: 3,300 m;

Bec des Rosses — iconic competition terrain

Bec des Rosses (~3,222 m) is internationally associated with the Xtreme
Verbier Freeride World Tour competition. It contains exposure, cliffs,
steep couloirs and competition-class terrain; it is not an ordinary
marked piste. In a game, treat this as a specialist expert challenge
zone, separate from resort wayfinding and maintained runs. Exact cliff
geometry and competition lines need survey/photogrammetry rather than a
broad 60° headline figure.

Critical freeride modelling rules

-   Treat natural moguls as irregular results of terrain and snow use,
    not repeated identical semicircles.

-   Localize exposure, slope direction, shade, rock outcrops, gullies,
    forest density and terrain traps.

-   Differentiate marked itinerary boundaries from unrestricted
    off-piste; include barriers and closures without claiming simulated
    conditions are a real-world avalanche forecast.

-   Keep route statistics as published metadata and derive actual
    elevation loss and horizontal distance from georeferenced geometry.

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Consistency caveat. Some published start/end altitudes do not
  numerically reconcile with the operator’s stated vertical drops. The
  registry intentionally preserves the source values; the game’s
  elevation profile must be computed independently from the DEM.

06 Snowpark, fun zones and race facilities

Verbier Snowpark — La Chaux

The principal snowpark sits around 2,250 m at La Chaux and is reached
via the Chaux-Express / Chaux 2 lift area. The published setup describes
two park entries and small (S), medium (M) and large (L) lines. The
small line includes five table-top/whoop features; medium/large layouts
include three kickers in the 9–13 m published range, together with
rails, tubes, boxes and other jib features. Shapes and precise obstacle
placements can change during the season.

Park geometry and safety in the simulation

-   Build every lip, tabletop, knuckle, landing transition, berm and
    obstacle as real collision geometry; no decorative-only kickers.

-   Calculate jump outcome from real board speed, takeoff geometry, body
    position, angular momentum and landing normal.

-   Maintain distinct park configurations by season/date because
    features are moved and rebuilt.

-   Add gates, fences, signs, maintenance vehicles and realistic human
    traffic to make the freestyle zone feel operational.

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Snow state                          Physics / visual outcome
  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------
  Fresh dry powder                    Depth resistance, sink, slow turns,
                                      powder plumes and track
                                      accumulation.

  Wind-packed snow                    Firm drifts, uneven support, wind
                                      ridges and changing surface drag.

  Breakable crust                     Surface failure below load,
                                      unexpected edge resistance and
                                      balance effects.

  Hardpack                            Fast glide, less surface
                                      deformation, edge-dependent grip.

  Refrozen irregular snow             Sharp vibration, variable traction
                                      and hard localized bumps.

  Wet spring snow                     Higher drag, heavy spray and sticky
                                      transitions.

  Moguls                              Repeated impacts, variable body

                                      momentum.

                                      resistance and realistic tipping
                                      moments.

Weather implementation

-   Use altitude-dependent precipitation, ambient temperature and wind;
    conditions can differ substantially between Verbier village and Mont
    Fort.

-   Calculate accurate solar direction, slope aspect, mountain
    shadowing, fog and cloud layers. North-facing La Tzoumaz/Tortin
    terrain can hold a different surface from sunny south-facing slopes.

-   Add piste-grooming resets, snowmaking where applicable, snow drift
    around buildings, boot/ski/snowboard tracks and temporal wear on
    high-traffic pistes.

-   Render distant silhouettes such as Grand Combin and Mont Blanc
    without incorrectly connecting them to the 4 Vallées lift network.

08 GIS sources and terrain import workflow

The user-provided panorama illustrates mountain relationships and marked
pistes, but it is not a planimetric map. A precise 3D recreation should
be constructed from Swiss mapping products, ski-feature linework and
field verification. Coordinates should be internally consistent (Swiss
LV95 / EPSG:2056 is an appropriate primary GIS space) before conversion
to engine-world metres.

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Dataset                 Practical use           Important caution
  ----------------------- ----------------------- -----------------------
  swissALTI3D             High-resolution terrain Use documented
                          elevation model (2 m    horizontal and vertical
                          domain, 0.5 m selected  reference systems; do
                          play zones).            not infer snow surface
                                                  from bare-earth DEM.

  SWISSIMAGE              Orthoimagery for        Image capture
                          buildings, roads, tree  year/resolution vary by
                          edges and land-cover    area.
                          alignment.              

  swissTLM3D              Structured landscape    Check type semantics
                          and transport feature   and coverage.
                          vectors.                

  OpenSkiData / OSM       Pistes and aerialway    Community-derived
                          paths in GIS-compatible geometry requires
                          formats.                current official-map
                                                  validation.

  Official operator map   Named routes, sector    Illustrated map is not
                          connectivity and        precise GIS geometry
                          statuses.               and commercial artwork
                                                  has separate rights.
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

Import pipeline

1.  Download the real elevation tiles and orthoimagery covering the core
    Verbier–4 Vallées mountain footprint.

2.  Convert to a consistent coordinate system; create an origin strategy
    and store real-world feature coordinates separately from
    floating-origin game transforms.

3.  Clip and tile elevation; create gameplay heightfields/meshes,
    physics collision and lower-detail distant render meshes.

4.  Import piste polygons/centre-lines and lift routes; validate each
    junction and station-to-slope transition.

5.  Fit buildings, mountain paths, vegetation coverage and snow masks to
    the surveyed aerial/vector layers.

6.  Author feature metadata (surface, difficulty, lift type, maintenance
    status, opening dates and source revision).

7.  Run slope/width/clearance tests, visual side-by-side comparisons and
    playable end-to-end navigation tests.

Precision and runtime

For a powerful local machine, prioritize full-resolution terrain in the
active rider vicinity, streamed 2 m domain tiles, and selective 0.5 m
detail around cliffs, snowparks and technical descents. Physics should
use localized, high-resolution colliders; a floating origin limits
numerical precision problems when traversing the domain. LOD/culling,
vegetation instancing and memory budgets still matter even with high-end
hardware.

Data structure recommendations

Every gameplay-relevant mountain feature should have a stable
identifier; a feature type; sector; canonical name; source and effective
date; geometry; numeric altitude and difficulty properties where
measured; current state; and uncertainty/status flags. Geometry gaps
should remain null, not be filled with false coordinates.

Suggested test criteria

-   Player can travel from Le Châble to key Verbier sectors through the
    correct lift sequence.

-   Every lift station connects plausibly to surrounding pistes,
    including correct uphill/downhill directions.

-   A trip from Mont Fort to Tortin follows the actual high mountain and
    valley corridor.

-   Freeride itineraries remain visibly separate from groomed pistes and
    respect their terrain character.

-   Snowpark air/landing physics respond to speed and launch angle
    rather than scripted animation alone.

-   All displayed map heights match the chosen DEM height reference
    within defined tolerances.

Definition of accurate

A release should only be labelled “geographically accurate” when key
piste and lift geometry is validated against georeferenced reference
data, the date of the lift network is explicit, and ambiguous or
approximate measurements remain identified as such. Photorealistic
textures alone do not make a map geographically correct.
